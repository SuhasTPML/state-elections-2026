The '''Fifteenth Legislative Assembly Election''' was held on 16 May 2016 for the 232 seats (except [[Thanjavur Assembly constituency|Thanjavur]] and [[Aravakurichi Assembly constituency|Aravakurichi]] for which held on 26 October 2016) of the [[Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly|Legislative Assembly]] in the state of [[Tamil Nadu]] in [[India]]. Jayalalithaa]] won the elections and became the first ruling party to be re-elected in Tamil Nadu since 1984, though with a simple majority.<ref>{{cite web | ==Background==
By the requirement, state assembly election must be held at an interval of five years or whenever it is dissolved by the [[President of India|president]]. The [[2011 Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly election|previous election]], to the 14th legislative assembly, was conducted 13 April 2011 and its term would have naturally expired on 22 May 2016. The election to the 15th legislative assembly was organised and conducted by the [[Election Commission of India]] (ECI) and was held in a single phase on 16 May 2016. There were over 5.79 crore voters in the electoral rolls and 65,616 polling stations in Tamil Nadu.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.business-standard.com/article/news-ians/over-5-79-crore-voters-in-tamil-nadu-116030401255_1.html|title=Over 5.79 crore voters in Tamil Nadu |work=Business Standard |date=4 March 2016}}</ref>

A special purification drive of electoral rolls between 15 and 29 February 2016 in all poll-bound States including Tamil Nadu was held, in which door-to-door verification was undertaken involving booth-level agents. On 12 February 2016, Election Commission of India announced that 17 assembly constituencies in Tamil Nadu will have [[Voter-verified paper audit trail]] (VVPAT) machines attached along with [[Indian voting machines|EVM]]s.<ref>{{Cite news|url=http://www.thehindu.com/news/national/tamil-nadu/kancheepuram-voters-to-have-vvpat-facility/article8319361.ece|title=Kancheepuram voters to have VVPAT|newspaper=[[The Hindu]]|access-date=6 May 2016}}</ref> [[Voter-verified paper audit trail]] (VVPAT) machines will be in place in 4,000 booths.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.newindianexpress.com/cities/chennai/Voter-Paper-Audit-at-4K-Booths-for-Polls/2016/01/30/article3251887.ece |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160131081922/http://www.newindianexpress.com/cities/chennai/Voter-Paper-Audit-at-4K-Booths-for-Polls/2016/01/30/article3251887.ece |url-status=dead |archive-date=31 January 2016 |title=Voter Paper Audit at 4K Booths for Polls |date=30 January 2016 |work=The New Indian Express}}</ref>

==Demographics==
At the 2011 India census, Tamil Nadu had a population of 7,21,47,030. A total of 1,44,38,445 people constituting 20.01% of the total population belonged to [[Scheduled Castes]] (SC) and 7,94,697 people constituting 1.10% of the population belonged to [[Scheduled tribes]] (ST).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.censusindia.gov.in/2011census/hlo/pca/pca_pdf/PCA-CRC-0000.pdf|title=SC/ST population in Tamilnadu 2011}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=http://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/Half-of-Indias-dalit-population-lives-in-4-states/articleshow/19827757.cms|title=Half of India's Dalit population lives in India's 4 states- Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal, Bihar and Tamil Nadu}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.thehindu.com/news/national/tamil-nadu/dalit-consolidation-in-state-a-far-cry/article7898316.ece|title=Dalit consolidation in state|newspaper=[[The Hindu]]|access-date=24 March 2016}}</ref> [[Other Backward Classes]] (OBCs) form 68% of the population.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://articles.economictimes.indiatimes.com/2016-01-10/news/69653751_1_m-karunanidhi-dravidian-thevars|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160131061556/http://articles.economictimes.indiatimes.com/2016-01-10/news/69653751_1_m-karunanidhi-dravidian-thevars|url-status=dead|archive-date=31 January 2016|title=OBC community in focus before Tamil Nadu assembly polls|newspaper=[[The Economic Times]]|date=10 January 2016}}</ref> As per the religious census of 2011, Tamil Nadu had 87.6% [[Hindus]], 5.9% [[Muslims]], 6.1% [[Christians]], 0.1% [[Jainism|Jains]] and 0.3% following other religions or no religion.<ref name=religion2011>{{cite web|title=Population By Religious Community – Tamil Nadu|year=2011|url=http://www.censusindia.gov.in/2011census/C-01.html|format=XLS|publisher=Office of The Registrar General and Census Commissioner, Ministry of Home Affairs, Government of India|access-date=13 September 2015}}</ref>

==Campaign to vote==
To ensure that the electorate exercise their right to vote, the [[Election Commission of India]] (ECI) launched their own campaign in Tamil Nadu. The Chief Electoral Officer for the Tamil Nadu assembly elections, [[Rajesh Lakhoni]], utilised social media platforms by creating memes and tweets themed around films, which have a huge reach in Tamil Nadu. Additionally, the Election Commission also recruited media personalities [[Suriya]], [[R Ashwin|Ashwin]] and [[Dinesh Karthik|Karthik]], whose campaigning videos were filmed and shared on social media.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.firstpost.com/bollywood/suriya-r-ashwin-dinesh-karthik-create-awareness-for-upcoming-tamil-nadu-elections-2669098.html |title=Suriya, R Ashwin, Dinesh Karthik create awareness for upcoming Tamil Nadu elections |work=Firstpost |date=11 March 2016 |author=Sreedhar Pillai}}</ref> The Election Commission also partnered with Twitter(X) for the first time in India, to boost its ''#TN100Percent'' hashtag campaign. Twitter users who have made use of the hashtag will be reminded to vote on the day of voting.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.thehindu.com/news/national/tamil-nadu/ec-tags-twitter-fortn100percent-drive/article8360080.ece |title=EC tags Twitter for #TN100percent drive |work=The Hindu |date=16 March 2016}}</ref> [[Twitter]] and [[Facebook]] collaborated with ECI in its awareness campaign on striving for 100 per cent voting in the polls.<ref>{{Cite news|url=http://articles.economictimes.indiatimes.com/2016-03-15/news/71543021_1_awareness-campaign-twitter-india-tamil-nadu-ceo|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160418070027/http://articles.economictimes.indiatimes.com/2016-03-15/news/71543021_1_awareness-campaign-twitter-india-tamil-nadu-ceo|url-status=dead|archive-date=18 April 2016|title=Awareness campaign by Twitter|newspaper=[[The Economic Times]]|access-date=6 May 2016}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news|url=http://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/politics-and-nation/after-twitter-election-commission-now-partners-with-facebook-for-tamil-nadu-polls/articleshow/51784795.cms|title=After Twitter, EC partners with Facebook|newspaper=[[The Economic Times]]|access-date=6 May 2016}}</ref>

==Candidates==
On 4 April, [[AIADMK]] released its candidate list for Tamil Nadu.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://infoelections.com/infoelection/index.php/tn/6314-tamil-nadu-aiadmk-candidate-list.html|title=ADMK candidate list|work=Infoelections.com}}</ref> On 14 April, [[Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam|DMK]] released its candidate list for Tamil Nadu.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://infoelections.com/infoelection/index.php/tn/6315-tamil-nadu-dmk-candidate-list.html|title=DMK candidate list|work=Infoelections.com}}</ref>

[[Gopi Shankar Madurai]], a 25 years old gender activist made a bid to contest as one of the youngest candidates in the Tamil Nadu Assembly election and also the first openly [[Intersex]] & [[Genderqueer]] person to do so. Gopi contested on the ticket of newly launched outfit Anaithu Makkal Munnetra Kazhagam for Madurai North. He secured 14th place out of 21 candidates.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.thehindu.com/todays-paper/tp-national/tp-tamilnadu/intersex-person-to-contest-from-madurai-north/article8539185.ece |title=Intersex person to contest from Madurai North – TAMIL NADU |work=The Hindu|date=30 April 2016 |access-date=22 May 2016}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/elections-2016/tamil-nadu-elections-2016/news/3rd-gender-gets-a-new-champion-in-Tamil-Nadu-poll-ring/articleshow/51935940.cms |title=3rd gender gets a new champion in Tamil Nadu poll ring |publisher=[[The Times of India]] |date=22 April 2016 |access-date=22 May 2016}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/chennai/Intersex-candidate-alleges-harassment/articleshow/52197962.cms |title=Intersex candidate alleges harassment  |publisher=[[The Times of India]] |date=10 May 2016 |access-date=22 May 2016}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.thenewsminute.com/article/intersex-person-contesting-tn-polls-ze-wants-change-your-mind-sexual-minorities-42103 |title=This intersex person is contesting TN polls, "ze" wants to change your mind on sexual minorities |publisher=The News Minute |date=24 April 2016 |access-date=22 May 2016}}</ref>

==Voter Turnout==
Voter turnout for the 232 constituencies on 16 May was 74.81%.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.thehindu.com/news/national/tamil-nadu/more-women-voted-in-tn-than-men-final-turnout-7426/article8611646.ece?ref=sliderNews |title=2016 Tamil Nadu Assembly Elections: More women voted than men, final turnout 74.26% |work=The Hindu|date=17 May 2016 |access-date=22 May 2016}}</ref> Parts of Tamil Nadu received heavy rain on the polling day, but it did not deter voters from turning out to cast their votes. While the highest voter turn out was in Dharmapuri constituency recording 85% while the lowest voter turnout was in the capital of Tamil Nadu, Chennai recording 55%. Male voter turnout was 74.15% and female voter turnout was 74.33%.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.dinamalar.com/news/tamil-nadu-news/news/1523508|title=தமிழகத்தில் 73.76 சதவீதம் ஓட்டுப்பதிவு: தேர்தல் ஆணையம்|website=Dinamalar|access-date=16 May 2016}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news|url=http://www.thehindu.com/elections/tamilnadu2016/live-tamil-nadu-assembly-polls-2016-tn-goes-to-polls/article8603673.ece?homepage=true|title=73.76% votes polled in TN|date=16 May 2016|newspaper=The Hindu|language=en-IN|issn=0971-751X|access-date=16 May 2016}}</ref>

==Controversies==
On 3 May, News 7 and [[Dinamalar]] released an opinion poll giving an edge for DMK over AIADMK. But some editions of Dinamalar carried a statement on the first page distancing itself from the surveys.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.thenewsminute.com/article/tn-polls-dinamalar-distances-itself-dinamalar-news-7s-west-tn-surveys-42655|title=TN polls: Dinamalar distances itself from Dinamalar-News 7's West TN surveys|date=4 May 2016|website=The News Minute|access-date=5 May 2016}}</ref> In an instance VVPAT was helpful in resolving an issue pertaining to a tally of votes in [[Kancheepuram Assembly constituency]] as the number of votes entered in the Form 17C of a polling booth and the total number of votes recorded in the [[Indian voting machines|EVM]] control unit of that booth did not tally.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.thehindu.com/news/national/tamil-nadu/vvpat-saves-the-day/article8622987.ece|title=VVPAT saves the day}}</ref>

===Postponement of polls===
The Election Commission postponed the polls in [[Aravakurichi Assembly constituency|Aravakurichi]] and [[Thanjavur Assembly constituency|Thanjavur]] constituencies due to reports of distribution of huge sums of money and alcohol to voters. The polling was initially postponed to 13 June, despite the [[Governor of Tamil Nadu]] requesting that it should happen before 1 June. The date was subsequently amended to November.{{citation needed|date=May 2018}}
