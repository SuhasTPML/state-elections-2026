The '''2016 Kerala Legislative Assembly election''' was held on 16 May 2016 to elect 140 [[MLA (India)|MLAs]] to the 14th [[Kerala Legislative Assembly]]. [[File:Legislative assembly constituencies in Kerala.svg|thumbnail|The constituencies to which election was held]] Voter turnout was 77.53%, up from 75.12% in the previous election.<ref>{{cite news |title=Final voter turnout in Kerala is 77.53 percent |url=http://www.abplive.in/india-news/final-voter-turnout-in-kerala-is-77-35-percent-343482 |publisher=ABP Live |date=17 May 2016 |access-date=17 May 2016 |archive-date=19 May 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160519005804/http://www.abplive.in/india-news/final-voter-turnout-in-kerala-is-77-35-percent-343482 |url-status=dead}}</ref> The result was declared on 19 May 2016. The [[Left Democratic Front (Kerala)|Left Democratic Front (LDF)]], led by [[Communist Party of India (Marxist)]] (CPI(M)), won the election, defeating the incumbent [[United Democratic Front (Kerala)|United Democratic Front (UDF)]], led by the [[Indian National Congress]] (INC), which could only win 47 seats in the election.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.hindustantimes.com/assembly-elections/live-assembly-poll-results-counting-of-votes-in-tamil-nadu-kerala-assam-west-bengal-puducherry/story-nmYc0zJVdyQ25jUFRZsGrN.html |title=As it happened: TMC, AIADMK retain power; BJP takes Assam, Left Kerala |date=19 May 2016 |newspaper=Hindustan Times |access-date=11 August 2019}}</ref> [[Pinarayi Vijayan]] was sworn in as the Chief Minister on 25 May.

== Background ==
The tenure of the members of the Legislative Assembly in the state was to end on 31 May 2016.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.elections.in/upcoming-elections-in-india.html |title=Upcoming Elections in India |access-date=9 November 2015}}</ref>
As per the voters list published on 14 January 2016, there were around 2.60 crore (26&nbsp;million) eligible voters including 6.18 lakh (618,000) new voters in the age group 18–21. Elections to the 140-member assembly were held in 21,498 polling stations set up at 12,038 locations.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/national/model-code-of-conduct-comes-into-force-in-kerala/article8314579.ece |title=Model code of conduct comes into force in Kerala |website=@businessline |date=4 March 2016}}</ref> There were 500 model polling stations.<ref name="Business Standard 2016">{{Cite web |url=https://www.business-standard.com/article/news-ians/2-56-crore-voters-to-cast-ballot-in-kerala-ec-116020401247_1.html |title=2.56 crore voters to cast ballot in Kerala: EC |date=4 February 2016 |via=Business Standard}}</ref> Systematic Voters' Education and Electoral Participation (SVEEP) programme was undertaken in Kerala to raise voter awareness.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.thehindu.com/news/cities/Kochi/higher-turnout-attributed-to-sveep/article8609546.ece |title=Higher turnout attributed to SVEEP |date=17 May 2016 |access-date=23 April 2019 |website=The Hindu}}</ref>

== Parties and coalitions ==
There are two major political coalitions in Kerala. The [[Left Democratic Front (Kerala)|Left Democratic Front]] (LDF) is the coalition of the left-wing and far-left parties, led by the [[Communist Party of India (Marxist)]] (CPI(M)). The [[United Democratic Front (Kerala)|United Democratic Front]] (UDF) is the coalition of centrist and centre-left parties led by the [[Indian National Congress]].
